# Coast TT — functional and technical specification

Status: planning baseline, not an implementation claim. Updated 2026-11-15.
Product decisions: [intent.md](intent.md). Execution: [tasks.md](tasks.md). Acceptance: [test.md](test.md). Operations: [deployment.md](deployment.md).

## Requirement conventions

REQ-001 through REQ-055 are authoritative requirements. Each maps to the same-numbered EVAL in test.md and at least one task in tasks.md. Phase 1 is the launch boundary; later-phase contracts reserve integration points without activating unfinished features.

### REQ-001

**Application architecture — foundation.** Launch one mobile-first React/Vite TypeScript PWA with Tailwind CSS, shadcn/ui, TanStack Query, Django/DRF API, PostgreSQL, Django Channels/Redis, Celery/Redis, IndexedDB for offline score persistence, and private S3-compatible attachments. Use compatible stable package versions and commit lockfiles when scaffolding. React Native/Expo and NativeWind belong to a later native follow-on, not the launch build.

Proposed repository: `apps/web`, `services/api`, `packages/contracts`, `packages/scoring`, `packages/design-tokens`, and `infra`; add `apps/mobile` only in the native follow-on. These directories are planned, not currently present. Keep Django domain modules inside one deployable service initially. Keep heavy media processing in separate workers later. Keep frontend types, pure scoring logic and tokens reusable by future native clients; Python independently validates accepted score streams against common fixtures.

### REQ-003

**Accounts and guests — Phase 1.** Verified email/password, sign-in/out, reset, and invitations. Resumable onboarding requires basic identity; extended profile and club discovery are skippable. Guests can be entered by staff and claim via verified single-use invitations. Name similarity alone never merges players. A conflicting existing identity requires staff resolution with an audit trail and preserved match references.

*Adding players before they register (user decision, 2026-11-20).* A tournament organizer or club admin (competitions or member staff of the host) adds a player who has no account yet, enters them, and invites them by email, phone or both. The invitation is a single-use link that lasts 14 days. We email it to an address, and staff share it by WhatsApp or SMS for a phone (no SMS is sent by the platform yet). Whoever opens the link sees where they were entered and either sets their own name, email and/or phone and password, or logs in to an existing account and claims the entries. When they set their own details, the guest becomes their account and nothing moves. An email or phone that matches the invitation counts as confirmed; a different email must be verified first. Players sign in with their email or their phone number. Phone numbers are stored in E.164 and are unique; numbers without a country code are read as Kenyan. A number a player adds themselves is not confirmed until SMS verification exists (TASK-118).

The PWA uses secure HttpOnly session cookies with CSRF protection. Revoke sessions on logout/password reset; authenticate live connections without durable credentials in query strings. Implement uniform login throttling and non-enumerating recovery responses. Offline score capture remains local if a session expires; require reauthentication as the same user to synchronize. Later native clients use a separately tested short-lived access/rotating refresh flow with platform secure storage.

### REQ-004

**Organizations — Phase 1.** Register club, facility, or both. Creator becomes first admin. Players can join multiple organizations. Join requests are pending/approved/rejected; invited members explicitly accept. Public tournament entry is independent of host membership. Organization switching changes operational context, not the global player identity. Platform operator authority is separate from club roles.

*Adding members directly (user request, 2026-11-22).* Besides invitations, club staff with members.manage can add someone directly by name and an email, a phone or both. The person is a member at once (shown to staff as "added by staff, hasn't signed in yet") and gets a single-use link, valid 14 days, by email, or shared by staff over WhatsApp or SMS. From it they set a password and sign in. Somebody already on Coast TT signs in from the same link and the membership moves to their account (REQ-003 guest claim; a clash goes to staff). Adding answers the same whether or not the address is registered. Tournament staff add entrants the same way: they pick registered players from the directory, and anyone else gets a guest entry and the same link (REQ-003).

*Finding a club (user decision, 2026-11-19).* Signed-in players find clubs by name, country, region and city, and open a club's profile before joining: location and directions, its venues in use, a description, weekly training hours and its published tournaments that have not ended. Nothing else (members, roles, training plans, drills, finances) is shown until they join. Club staff with roles.manage edit the description; training.manage edits the training hours.

*Finding clubs and places nearby (user request, 2026-11-25).* The club finder and the facility finder (REQ-044) share one search: a single box that matches a name, city or town; a "Choose a region or city" option for the country, region and city lists; and **Near me**, which asks the device for its position and lists the clubs or facility branches within 300 m, 1 km, 5 km, 10 km or 25 km, nearest first, each with its distance. Only places with a map position appear in a distance search, and the facility finder's visibility rules apply unchanged. The player's position is personal data: it is sent in a request body (`POST /organizations/nearby/`, `POST /facilities/nearby/`), never in a URL, is not stored or logged by the API, is not saved on the device, and the search is rate-limited per player (`THROTTLE_NEARBY`, default 30 a minute). A distance search around a named city is not offered: the city catalogue holds no coordinates.

*Menu, Home and help (user request, 2026-11-25; extends REQ-002).* The menu has a **Tournaments** entry for everyone (`/tournaments`, open signed out): tournaments being played now, the player's entries, and the list to search; competition staff start a new tournament from it. The phone's bottom bar holds five entries (Home, Tournaments, Play, Club, then More). Home offers the common tasks as shortcuts. A help centre (`/help`, open signed out) answers how to enter and follow tournaments, join and register clubs, find places nearby, and run tournaments and facilities; it is searchable and each answer has its own link.

### REQ-005

**Governance — Phase 1.** Editable admin/captain/assistant captain/committee/referee/member templates plus custom role names and permission bundles. Permission domains: members, roles, competitions, results, assigned scoring, facilities, finances, announcements, training, welfare projects, club activities, and later media/billing. Users may hold multiple roles; effective grants are the union within that organization. Granting authority cannot exceed the acting user's delegable permissions. Keep one active admin. Enforce checks on API reads/writes, live events, files, and UI actions.

Custom member fields: text, number, date, boolean, single selection; required/optional; member-visible or staff-only. Field definitions belong to an organization, values to its membership. Field access does not override other privacy rules. Preserve historical values when retiring a field.

### REQ-006

**Profiles and discovery — Phase 1.** Global display name, avatar, nickname, bio, hand, grip, style, self-described experience and optional goals. Goals affect shortcuts, not permissions or ratings. Club/facility search by name/town and opted-in player directory. Extended details are private until explicitly published. No precise player location or contact exposure. Equipment enrichment arrives under REQ-026.

### REQ-007

**Tournament setup — Phase 1.** Tournament has host, venue, dates/timezone, publication state and one or more singles/doubles/team events. Event setup includes format, capacity, scoring best-of, rest period, registration window, entry fee, seed order, groups and qualification count. Lifecycle draft → published → in progress → completed, with cancellation as audited terminal action. Separate public publication from operational permissions. Freeze format/qualification structure on start. Walkovers and withdrawals are explicit outcomes.

### REQ-008

**Registration — Phase 1.** Organizers choose open or closed admission for each event. During the registration window, open admission automatically accepts eligible self-entries while there is room and waitlists overflow. Closed admission sends self-entries to the host's competition staff, who approve, waitlist, or reject them. Closed is the default for existing events and new events with no mode selected. Staff may place entries directly from the entry desk. The admission mode can change until the event starts and applies to new entries. The registration window controls when players can submit entries.

Track pending, accepted, waitlisted, rejected, and withdrawn entries. Track check-in separately. Enforce capacity transactionally when creating or approving entries. Doubles entries require two distinct players. Team entries require a roster and captain. Each player can have one standing entry per event. Phase 1 lets organizers declare an entry fee. Phase 2 adds detailed receipts. Acceptance grants a place and payment is tracked separately. Staff promote waitlisted entries after rechecking capacity and notify the players.

### REQ-009

**Formats and draw publication — Phase 1.** Round robin, single elimination and groups-to-knockout, applicable to player/pair/team entries. Preview draw with seeds and explicit byes; publish a version. Tie matches are distinct from their individual constituent matches. Derive knockout participants from official qualified results. Corrections follow REQ-015. Capture the competition rules version with the event.

### REQ-010

**Tournament tabs and standings — Phase 1.** Always provide Brackets, Groups, Schedule, Standings. Brackets show rounds/byes/scores/current entry; Groups show members/fixtures/qualification; Schedule shows day/table/status and player filters; Standings explain rankings and tie-breaks. Non-applicable tabs explain the format. Offer a round-list alternative to a horizontally scrollable bracket. A public match detail never exposes an unauthorized Enter score control.

Before implementing qualification, transcribe the applicable ITTF group ranking rules into versioned fixtures: match points, tied-subgroup comparisons, game/point ratios, repeat subgroup resolution and ultimate unresolved-tie procedure. Cite the adopted clauses and explicitly represent unplayed/withdrawn matches. This rules-validation task is a prerequisite to draw/standings coding, not a runtime choice delegated to users. A shared exact-ratio comparator avoids rounded-ratio ties. Do not assign arbitrary unfinished knockout rankings.

### REQ-011

**Team ties — Phase 1.** Organizer defines an ordered template of singles/doubles rubbers and required wins; each side submits an eligible lineup before a tie begins. A rubber is an individual match with its own scoring/rating. Validate distinct partners and roster membership; player reuse across sequential rubbers is allowed. Run one rubber at a time per tie by default and stop when the required wins are reached. Unneeded rubbers remain not played and do not affect ratings. Reserve active rubber participants, not every team member for the entire tie.

### REQ-012

**Scoring — Phase 1.** Assigned umpire controls point entry, undo, initial server/receiver, end changes, one timeout per side, event log, and confirmation. Default best of five; allow three/seven; games to 11 with two-point margin. Rotate service every two points, every point after 10–10, and implement doubles order and deciding-game receiving/end changes. Undo reverses the last action through an audit-preserving compensating event. Explicit walkover/retirement flows cannot fabricate normal completed scores. Assigned umpire confirmation becomes official only after server validation. Timeout timing, penalty cards, injury suspensions, the expedite system, walkovers, retirements and disqualifications follow REQ-043.

### REQ-013

**Offline assigned scoring — Phase 1.** Download full assigned-match state before disconnection. Persist each score event in an IndexedDB transaction before acknowledging it in the UI. Event envelope includes unique ID, match ID, browser/device assignment ID, sequence, expected server revision, event type and device time. Device time assists media but is not authority for result ordering. Retry safely, deduplicating IDs and rejecting gaps/stale authority. One active device assignment per match; organizer reassignment invalidates old authority. Conflicts remain visible for organizer review, never last-write-wins. Pending finish reserves players/table until accepted. Reopening restores the deck and pending events. Global scheduling and progression require connectivity; this is not a venue-wide offline server.

Cache the versioned app shell through a service worker so the downloaded match reopens offline. Use account/assignment-scoped IndexedDB records and single-writer ownership across browser tabs; sequence allocation and ownership checks are transactional. On focus/reconnect, reread local and server revision before writing. Keep authenticated API responses, finances and private notes out of generic service-worker caches. Handle quota/write errors before displaying a score as saved. Request persistent storage where supported and show actionable storage failure; browser/user eviction cannot be guaranteed against. Reconnect/open/manual retry are mandatory; background sync is optional. Logout/account switching must protect pending scores through sync or an explicit resolution flow before purging account-local records.

### REQ-014

**Dispatch and reservations — Phase 1, extended in Phases 2/3.** Central reservation model covers all participating players and physical tables across events/organizations. At dispatch lock the relevant table/player resource records in stable order, recheck eligibility and create reservations in one database transaction. Future approved booking/training ranges cannot overlap; active matches block dispatch even after estimated end. Check official predecessors, participant check-in and rest. Default rest ten minutes; configurable per event. Umpires assigned as players must not simultaneously be dispatched elsewhere. Estimate schedules without guaranteeing exact start times.

On availability/result changes, rank ready matches by planned start, then ready-since waiting duration, then stable ID. Offer manual dispatch and optional organizer-enabled automatic dispatch. Explain blocked/idle tables. Recheck at execution even after a suggestion was displayed. Override actions cannot violate a player/table exclusivity invariant.

### REQ-015

**Official results and corrections — Phase 1.** Server acceptance atomically records the official result, releases active reservations, and writes durable progression/rating/notification work. Projection jobs are idempotent and may show processing status. Admin correction records author, reason, prior and replacement result. Invalidate/rebuild dependent projections. If a changed winner affects started downstream play, pause the affected event and require explicit resolution; never silently replace an opponent. Preserve history instead of deleting it.

### REQ-016

**Ratings — Phase 1.** Separate global singles/doubles Elo. Start 1000, K32, divisor 400. Expected score = 1/(1+10^((opponent-rating)/400)); delta = 32 × (actual−expected). For doubles compare pair averages; each partner receives its side's delta. Keep precision, round only for display. Rate normal completed competitive individual matches, including team rubbers; exclude casuals, walkovers, retirements/abandoned matches, disqualifications, unplayed rubbers and aggregate ties. Jumbles matches are rated in the doubles ledger only at an official tournament (regional, national or international, approved by an official); a club's local Jumbles is unrated (owner decision, 2026-11-18). Keep immutable rating-event order based on first official server acceptance plus stable ID; correction replay preserves that order. Expose before/after values, recent form, match count and win rate with explicit filters. Rank labels identify singles/doubles and platform/club scope; provisional processing is visible.

### REQ-017

**Quick Score — Phase 1.** Unrated singles/doubles scoring independent of tournaments. Local guest use allowed; authenticated players may save casual records. Claimed participant names do not grant permission to edit another player's profile or official history. Clearly label recorder-entered casual history. Casual results do not alter Elo/standings and do not create physical reservations.

### REQ-018

**Recaps and awards — Phase 1.** Result, games, participants, and competitive rating change; branded downloadable cards and public share links for published competitions. Mark processing if rating projection is pending. Derive placements; organizer selects discretionary awards and publishes winners announcement. Do not infer aces/rally lengths/shot types from score presses. Link to private analysis when Phase 3 is available.

### REQ-019

**Notifications — Phase 1 onward.** Persistent inbox with read state, category preferences and optional browser push where supported. Feature-detect push/install capabilities and explain device-specific setup; the inbox remains available when push is unsupported or denied. Native push is deferred to the native follow-on. Triggers: next eligible queue position, table call, changed/cancelled assignment, registration decision, announcement/awards; later booking/dues/highlights. Distinguish Up next from Called to table. Version assignments and deduplicate by recipient/event/version/channel. Cancel stale pending push jobs; previously delivered alerts resolve to current state. Push is best effort; inbox is durable. Broadcast audience is explicit (organization, event or named participants).

### REQ-020

**Public and private boundaries — all phases.** Published competition pages show limited names, ratings, draws/results and awards. Public serializers use explicit allowlists. Private data includes contacts, notes, staff fields, finances, training commitments, and unpublished media. Player directory visibility does not override public competition records. Signed attachments/playback require authorization at issue time and short expiry. Revoked access blocks new links; already downloaded public media cannot be recalled. Use named test users and organizations in privacy tests.

### REQ-021

**Facility bookings — Phase 2.** Opening hours, closures, table slots, fees, and request/approve/reject/cancel lifecycle. Pending requests do not block capacity; approval atomically checks REQ-014. Organizer sees requested and approved states distinctly. Cancellation releases future reservations; completed bookings stay in history. Fees and cancellation adjustments are recorded manually and auditably; no automatic online refund promise.

### REQ-022

**Payments — Phase 2.** Record currency, integer minor-unit amount, method (cash/external), received date, reference, payer, recorder and allocations. Allocate to event fees, booking charges or dues. Prevent over-allocation; keep unmatched excess as visible credit pending allocation. Receipt references assist reconciliation but are not universally unique payment identities. Reverse erroneous records with reason and recompute balances; retain original. No payment processing in launch phases.

### REQ-023

**Contributions — Phase 2.** Multiple purpose campaigns with title/description, amount, selected members and one-time/weekly/monthly/quarterly/yearly cadence. Snapshot each generated due; idempotent generation key campaign/member/period. Monthly dates clamp to the final valid day; edits affect future ungenerated periods. Track pending/partial/paid/overdue/waived; partial overdue amounts remain visibly overdue. Members see their own dues and purpose totals. Authorized staff record waivers with reasons. Joining a club does not retroactively create old dues. Club operations dues are the standard case (user decision, 2026-11-15): a club can run a weekly or monthly "Club operations" campaign (offered as a ready-made template) that all active members owe by default, with per-member amount overrides or waivers (e.g. juniors, honorary members), and its REQ-024 expenses cover running costs such as hall/table hire, balls, nets, repairs and utilities so members see what their dues paid for. New members start owing from the next period after joining; leaving stops future dues but keeps earlier ones.

### REQ-024

**Spending transparency — Phase 2.** Expense purpose, amount, date, description and private receipt. Retain reversals/corrections. Show actual received allocations, actual expenses, and remaining funds separately from expected dues. Financial staff can inspect individual balances; members see own balances and shared purpose summaries. Expenses may exceed receipts but must visibly show the deficit; no silent invented funds. Separate campaign balances from platform subscription revenue.

### REQ-025

**Match analysis — Phase 3.** Player-owned private notes linked to a match, observations, improvement goals and timestamps. Only the owning player can read/edit through normal product APIs; club roles do not grant note access. A coach the player has accepted sees the player's matches and writes their own notes for the player under REQ-047; the player's own notes stay theirs alone. Casual and competitive match links supported. For official (umpire-confirmed) matches the player may add and edit only these private notes; the official sets, scores and result are read-only to them (user decision, 2026-10-03). Self-recorded matches are covered by REQ-037.

### REQ-026

**Equipment and drills — Phase 3.** Optional blade/forehand/backhand free-text equipment. Personal private and club-visible drill libraries with instructions, focus, duration, repetitions and equipment. Owners edit personal drills; training permission edits club drills. Equipment data makes no automatic performance claim.

### REQ-027

**Training — Phase 3.** Personal plans with completion tracking and scheduled commitments. Staff create club sessions, reserve tables, invite members and record attendance. An invitation does not reserve a player's time until acceptance; accepted commitments use REQ-014. A non-calendar checklist is not a reservation. Unavailable table/player produces conflict feedback before confirmation.

### REQ-028

**Recording — Phase 4.** One featured table, fixed camera/phone and OBS laptop. Test 1080p50/60 local capture; outbound format depends on destination. Full table and both players in frame. Keep local recording independently of uplink; associate recording sessions/segments with table and match intervals. Model media-time-to-score-time offsets per continuous segment, recalibrating after reconnects. Capture status is distinct from social live status. Privacy, retention, and publication policy must be resolved before recording real participants.

### REQ-029

**Highlights — Phase 4.** Manual highlight markers plus score-derived candidate moments (deciding finishes, match-winning points, close scores). Store candidate start/end, source segment, event link and review state. Organizer adjusts boundaries and approves publication. Python/FFmpeg creates branded clips and tournament recaps using bounded background jobs. Idempotent processing, failed-job retry, player gallery and ready notification. AI later proposes candidates; it does not certify longest rally or other unmeasured statistics. Private recordings remain protected until approved publication.

### REQ-030

**Social broadcasting — Phase 5.** Coast TT controls selected destinations while OBS supplies the feed. Managed relay provisionally Cloudflare Stream; current capability/cost confirmation is a prerequisite. Facebook/Instagram/TikTok connections are conditional on actual account access; support supplied URL/key workflows and external confirmation where required. Encrypt secrets and audit control actions without logging keys. Separate selected/enabled/receiving/live/failed states; show platform confirmation uncertainty. Keep external watch links in app, not a required app-hosted audience. Portrait composition must preserve both players. Local capture continues when a destination fails. No broadcast is started by merely saving settings.

### REQ-031

**Organization subscriptions — Phase 6.** Organizations pay for platform plans; club member contributions are separate. Plan prices, provider, entitlement matrix, usage units, trial/grace/cancellation/refund/tax policies and downgrade behavior require approval before implementation. Preserve tenant data through expiry; avoid abruptly terminating active tournament scoring. Later contract includes organization subscription, versioned entitlements, invoice/provider references, usage records and idempotent billing events. Never display unapproved prices as commercial offers.

### REQ-032

**API contracts and security — foundation.** Version `/api/v1/`; OpenAPI types; UUID resource IDs; UTC ISO timestamps; explicit pagination/filters; money `{amount_minor,currency}`; errors carry code/message/field details and request ID. Resource groups: auth, organizations/memberships/roles/fields, players, tournaments/events/entries, matches/score-events/results, reservations/bookings, ratings, notifications, contributions/payments/expenses, training, and later media/subscriptions. Expected-revision conflict uses HTTP 409. Idempotency keys scope to actor/operation and validate payload reuse. Public read endpoints return only published fields. Validate attachments, rates and object ownership server-side. Store no credentials in source or public web bundles.

### REQ-033

**Reliability and observability — all phases.** PostgreSQL owns durable state and outbox; Redis is not the only copy of work. Atomic mutations enqueue outbox records; workers retry with dedupe and failed-job inspection. One scheduler prevents duplicate periodic dispatch. Track API health, latency/errors, job age/failures, disk, push errors and backup freshness without sensitive payload logging. Launch load target: eight tables, 100 simultaneous app connections, 15-minute steady test; core API p95 <500ms and score visibility p95 <2s in controlled conditions. These are acceptance targets, not measured capacity claims.

### REQ-034

**Hosting/recovery — foundation and launch.** Full ASGI/worker deployment on Contabo or Namecheap VPS. Namecheap shared hosting can serve React static assets with API on VPS. Use deployment.md for configuration, least-exposed network, staging, backup, restore, migration and rollback. Target RPO 15 minutes and RTO four hours, demonstrated by restore, not inferred from backup success. Test off-site database and attachment recovery; snapshots supplement backups.

### REQ-035

**PWA installation/update compatibility — foundation and launch.** HTTPS, manifest with name/icons/start URL/scope/display/theme, versioned app-shell service worker and offline fallback. Validate Android and iOS browser/home-screen installation experiences with capability-aware instructions. Expose an update-available action; defer activating a new service worker/reloading while a match is active or unsynced events exist. Migrate IndexedDB without discarding pending events. API remains compatible with cached older PWA bundles; handle unsupported-client versions without deleting queued scores. An install is not required for public pages or online use. No app-store signing/EAS pipeline in launch. **Working offline (user decision, 2026-11-21):** the app works offline and syncs when the connection returns. Screens a signed-in person has opened are saved on the device for their account, shown offline with when they were saved, and removed when another account signs in or at logout. Finances, door takings, private notes, coaching records, invitation links and guest records are not saved and need a connection. Changes that happen at the venue (meeting check-in and attendance, club session attendance, tournament self check-in, logging a match) are kept on the device when there is no connection and sent in order when it returns. Each is sent with an Idempotency-Key, so a retry after a lost reply is never applied twice. A change the server refuses is set aside with its reason until the person retries or discards it, never dropped quietly. Sending pauses when the session has ended until the same person logs in again, and logging out with changes still on the device asks first. Other changes, and anything that depends on other people's live data (draws, payments, approvals), need a connection. Later native follow-on adds Expo build profiles, SQLite journal, secure tokens, push and store/update compatibility evaluations against the shared API.

### REQ-036

**Documentation and design gate — Phase 0.** Complete these five linked files, then generate/review Stitch screens before application code. Screen inventory records ID/title/device/phase/requirements and known gaps. Mark tasks complete only with evidence. Mock screenshots do not prove runtime behavior. Preserve future phases as designed/deferred, not launched. Content changes update linked tasks/evaluations in the same change.

### REQ-037

**Personal match log — Phase 1 (user decision, 2026-10-03).** An authenticated player can record matches they played outside official Coast TT scoring: date, a free-text label of their choosing (suggestions such as Friendly, Tournament, Practice, League; previously used labels offered again), singles/doubles, free-text opponent (and partner) names, optional venue, set-by-set points, and notes. The record is personal data owned by the recording player: only that player can list, read, edit or delete it; club roles, platform staff APIs, public pages, directories and other players never see it. Free-text names never link to, notify, or grant edit rights over another player's identity. Each set needs a winner; the match result is derived from sets won. Progress summaries (played/won/lost, win rate, by label, recent form) are computed only from the owner's records. Personal records never alter Elo, standings, official history, or reservations, and are always labeled as self-recorded. Matches scored officially on Coast TT are not re-entered here: their scores are read-only and the player adds private notes per REQ-025.

### REQ-038

**Welfare projects — Phase 2 (user decision, 2026-11-15).** Club staff with the welfare permission set up a welfare project for a shared cause, for example supporting a sick or bereaved member, with a title, purpose description, optional beneficiary member, suggested contribution per member (e.g. KES 50 as `{amount_minor,currency}`), optional target and deadline, and audience (all active members or selected members). Contributions are voluntary unless staff explicitly make the project a due under REQ-023; a suggested amount never creates an overdue status on its own. Contributions are recorded manually through the REQ-022 ledger (cash/external, no payment processing) and allocated to the project; spending such as a hospital visit gift uses REQ-024 expenses, so received, spent and remaining amounts reconcile and show deficits. Members see the project, the total raised against the target, and their own contribution; per-member amounts are visible only to financial staff unless the club opts to show a contributor list, and each contributor may stay anonymous in it. The beneficiary's health or personal circumstances are sensitive: staff write only what the beneficiary (or their family) agreed to share, the beneficiary can ask staff to hide the project, and no medical detail appears in notifications, public pages or directories. A project can include a planned visit or support activity (date, meeting point, who is going) using REQ-039 activities. Projects close manually or at the deadline; closing keeps the ledger and summary read-only.

### REQ-039

### REQ-040

**Soda games (friendly challenges) — Phase 1, stakes behind a policy gate (user decision, 2026-11-15).** A player challenges another player (same club or any player reachable through the opted-in directory) to a friendly singles or doubles match called a soda game. ### REQ-043

**Match-day operations — Phase 1 (user decision, 2026-11-15).** Tournament staff run the whole match day in Coast TT: schedule, tables, check-in, calling, live scoring, incidents, walkovers and results announcements. Referee is a tournament role granted through the competitions permission (REQ-005); umpires act only on matches assigned to them.

*Schedule.* Staff build each day as sessions (date, start time, tables, rounds/groups included) and the system estimates match times from best-of, estimated match length and rest (REQ-014). Staff move a match to another table or slot or reorder within a session; every move is rechecked against reservations and never violates player/table exclusivity. Publishing creates a versioned day schedule shown in the Schedule tab (REQ-010) with "times are venue local, estimates only"; changing a published match's table or time by more than a configurable threshold (default 15 minutes) notifies its players and umpire (REQ-019) and keeps the prior version in history. Delays (late start, table out of service) shift the estimates of the affected table's remaining matches.

*Control centre.* A desktop/tablet match-day board shows every table (free, called, warming up, in play, blocked with reason), the ready queue, matches awaiting a called player, and live scores. From it staff check players in, assign or reassign umpires and tables (REQ-013 device authority), call a match (REQ-019 "Called to table"), recall, and record no-shows. A called player who has not reported within the event's grace period (configurable, default 10 minutes) is flagged; only the referee can then award a walkover. Every staff action is audited with actor, time and reason.

*Live scoring in real time.* Accepted score events reach public and member live views (tournament page, match detail, score strip, REQ-042 live badge) within the REQ-033 target (p95 under 2 seconds in controlled conditions) over a server push channel, with polling fallback. Viewers see game score, games won, server, timeouts used, cards shown and match status; they never see umpire controls (REQ-010).

*Timeouts.* Each player or pair may take one timeout of up to one minute per individual match, requested by the player/pair or their adviser and recorded by the umpire. The deck shows a countdown and logs start and end; play resumes early when the requesting side is ready. A side cannot take a second timeout in the same match.

*Penalty cards.* Cards are explicit umpire actions with a required reason category (e.g. bad behaviour, racket abuse, illegal advice, delay) and optional note, logged as score events and shown in the live view. Player misbehaviour ladder per individual match: first offence yellow card (warning); second offence yellow+red, one penalty point to the opponent; third offence yellow+red, two penalty points to the opponent; a further offence suspends play and calls the referee, who may disqualify (red card). Adviser misbehaviour: yellow card warning, then red card sending the adviser away for the rest of the match (team event: the rest of the tie). Penalty points are scoring events that obey normal game and service rules and can end a game or match. In a team tie, warnings and penalties carry across that tie's rubbers. The referee can also disqualify directly for serious misconduct. Exact carry-over details and the ladder are encoded in the TASK-003 reviewed rules fixtures before implementation; configuration may not weaken them for sanctioned events. **Who sees what (user decision, 2026-11-22):** a card's reason category is public with the card, in the live view and the match log; the umpire's note is for the tournament's staff only.

*Injury suspension and expedite.* The referee can grant a suspension for accidental incapacity of up to 10 minutes, with a countdown; a player who cannot resume retires. The expedite system can be started by the umpire or referee under the rules fixtures (time limit in a game, with the receiver winning the point after the required number of returns); while active the deck shows the return count and alternates service accordingly.

*Walkovers, retirements, disqualifications.* Outcomes are explicit and never fabricate scores: walkover (W/O) with reason (no-show after grace, withdrawal before start, injury before start), recorded with no game scores; retirement (ret.) keeps the points played and awards the match; disqualification (DQ) keeps the points played, records the referee's decision and can remove the player from later matches in the event. All three are excluded from Elo (REQ-016), propagate to brackets and groups per the rules fixtures, and are audited; corrections follow REQ-015.

*Results announcements.* Staff generate a results announcement for a session, round, day, event or the whole tournament. The generator builds a draft only from official results: each match with both sides in the same first/second order as the match card (REQ-002 left/right), games won and per-game scores (e.g. "3-1 (11-7, 9-11, 11-5, 11-8)"), W/O, ret. and DQ marked, winners and podium/placements (REQ-018), group standings where applicable, and outstanding matches listed as pending. It never invents commentary, shot descriptions or rating values not yet projected. Staff edit the draft text, preview it as a plain-text post (for WhatsApp and social media) and a branded image card, and publish it to the tournament news feed (members or public according to the tournament's publication state), with optional notification (REQ-019) and copy/share actions. Publishing freezes the version; a later result correction marks the post outdated and offers an amended version that keeps the original visible.

### REQ-045

**Governance structure — Phase 1 (user decision, 2026-11-19).** Clubs, regions and the country each have a committee whose positions and fields are configurable, and clubs send representatives up to their region.

*Club admin.* Each club has exactly one nominated club admin, who holds every club permission (the REQ-005 Admin role) and is the authority over the club's settings, roles, committee and nominations; other members edit only what the club admin delegates through roles. The creator is the first club admin. The club admin nominates a successor, who must accept; the handover is audited and the previous admin keeps only the roles they still hold. If the club admin leaves or is unreachable, a platform super admin can appoint one on request, with a recorded reason. A club never has zero or two club admins.

*Committees.* The club admin, or anyone they delegate the governance permission to, sets the committee: positions (for example chair, secretary, treasurer, organizing secretary, youth officer) with names, order, optional term start and end, and custom fields per position or per holder (text, number, date, choice, as REQ-005 member fields). Each position is held by an active member and may grant a role, so a treasurer can get the finance permission. Past holders stay in the committee history. Members see the current committee; custom fields follow their own visibility.

*Regional and national bodies.* Each region and the country has a governing body, an organization of kind `regional_body` or `national_body` tied to one catalogue region or country (TASK-093). A platform super admin creates the body and nominates its first admin; from then on it runs like a club: configurable positions, custom fields, roles and its own admin. Its members are its officials and committee, not players. Officials are the body's members holding the approval permission (`tournaments.approve`, decision D4), which the body's Official role carries and which no club role can hold: in a region's body they approve regional publication, and in the country's body national publication (REQ-007 scopes, TASK-094). The individual appointments that came before TASK-107 were moved into these bodies (each appointee became a member of the matching body, its admin if the body was made for them, otherwise an Official) and are kept read-only as history. A national body works for every club of its country as a regional body does for its region (REQ-048).

*Representatives.* A club in a region nominates one or more representatives (active members) to its regional body; a regional body nominates representatives of its regions to the national body. The receiving body sees nominations, accepts or declines, and can set a cap per club. A representative belongs to the body as a representative position with a term, can be replaced by the nominating club, and is removed automatically when they leave the club. A body sees a representative's display name and the club they represent, never their private profile details.

As built (TASK-108): the sender's governance staff (`governance.manage`) nominate an active member with an optional term; the receiving body's governance staff accept or decline, with an optional note, and may remove a representative later. The cap is one number per body, from 1 to 20 or none, applied to each sending organization; it counts nominations awaiting a decision and representatives in term, and is checked again at acceptance. The sender withdraws a nomination, recalls a representative, or nominates a replacement, who takes over when accepted without needing an extra place. A term that has ended frees the place. Leaving the sending organization ends the place and withdraws a pending nomination. Every row is kept as history. A representative is not a member of the body and holds no permission in it; the nominee is told but is not asked to accept, since the decision is the receiving body's. A club's members see who represents them; a body's members see its current representatives.

### REQ-046

**Meetings and non-competition events — Phase 1 (user decision, 2026-11-19).** A club, regional body or national body schedules meetings and events that are not competitions.

*Meetings.* A meeting has title, kind (committee, general, representatives, special), start and end in the host's timezone, venue or online link, invitees (committee, representatives, all members, or named people) and an agenda of ordered items, each with an optional presenter and attachment (REQ-020 private files). The agenda is shared with invitees when the meeting is published and can be revised before it starts; invitees are notified of publication, changes and cancellation (REQ-019) and RSVP. When the meeting starts, invitees check themselves in as present while it is in progress, or the secretary marks them present, late, apologies or absent; the attendance record is kept with time. Invitees see the rules when the meeting is published. From then on the attendance mark is fixed and only the host's admin (the club, facility or body admin) or a platform super admin changes a penalty: its wording, fine, status (issued, waived, paid) and a note, each change kept with who made it and when. A penalized invitee sees their own penalty; staff and the secretary see everyone's. Fines are recorded, not collected.

*Events that are not competitions.* Any level hosts events such as workshops, coaching clinics, anniversaries, award nights and open days, built on REQ-039 activities. Capacity, RSVP and waitlist follow REQ-039. Check-in at the start marks attendance the same way as meetings.

*Privacy.* Meetings and minutes are visible only to invitees and the host's governance staff; a body never sees a club's internal meetings. Attendance lists show display names only.

As built (TASK-110): an activity's audience is all members, selected members, a body's representatives (accepted and in term, of the sending clubs chosen or all of them), named people (members of the host or players listed in the directory), or every signed-in player. Whoever the audience includes sees the event, answers and checks in; each person's list of such events is `/me/events/` (Events for you). Publishing tells the audience, except for an event open to all players, which tells nobody; changes and cancellation tell the audience, or for an open event those who answered going or maybe. While the event runs, a person in the audience checks in: that marks them there, and takes a place if they hadn't answered and one is free. Activities staff mark anyone on the list there or not once it has started, and may add someone from the audience who never answered, except at an open event. Who is going is shown by display name to the audience, and for an open event to its staff only; check-in times and attendance are for staff. Cost sharing and welfare visits stay with member audiences.

As built (TASK-109): a body also invites its representatives (those accepted and in term, REQ-045). Each agenda item has an id given by the API, so its documents (PDFs up to 10 MB, at most 10 a meeting, private files read by whoever sees the meeting), decisions and action items stay with it when the agenda is reordered; an item links only to one of the host's tournaments or a published one. Invitees get a notice (category Meetings) when a meeting is published, each time it changes before it starts, when it is cancelled, when a penalty is issued to them, and when minutes are approved or amended; whoever made the change isn't told. Governance staff or the secretary write the minutes as a draft once the meeting has started: text, decisions, action items (owner: an invitee or an active member of the host) and the next meeting's date. Corrections are amendments (at most 20) listed under the minutes. `/me/action-items/` lists a person's open items from approved minutes in every organization, soonest due first.

### REQ-051

External income is kept for good and reversed with a reason, like expenses. The totals for any dates give internal, external and their sum per currency, with external broken down by source. ### REQ-052

**Training rules and location check-in — Phase 1 (user request, 2026-11-21).** A club sets its training schedule (weekly slots with a usual training location: a venue or a map point) and a check-in window: how long before and after a session's start a player may check in. A late check-in is marked late.

*Location check-in.* Players check themselves in to training only through the app, and only while their device reports a position within 300 m of the session's location (the club may set a smaller radius). The check is made in the API from the position the device sends with its accuracy; a position less accurate than the radius is refused with a message to try again outdoors. The API keeps only the distance, accuracy and time of the check-in, never a track. Staff can still mark attendance by hand for a player without a working phone, and the record shows it was marked by staff.

*Location changes.* When a session is held somewhere other than usual, the club admin (or `training.manage`) sets that session's location by picking a venue or entering coordinates. Check-in for that session is measured from the new location, and invitees are told it has moved.

*Apologies.* A player sends an apology (with an optional reason) for a training session or a meeting before it starts. Staff see the apologies, and attendance shows the player as apologised rather than absent, for the penalty rules (REQ-054, REQ-046).

As built (TASK-126): an apology is sent before the start, for a training session through its own action and for a meeting as the "apologies" answer, each with an optional reason of up to 300 characters; after the start both are refused. A training apology frees the member's time (their invitation becomes declined) and is withdrawn by accepting again. Training staff, and for a meeting whoever takes attendance, see who apologised and why; other members and invitees don't. A member who apologised and is marked absent still shows as apologised. A plain "can't come" is not an apology.

As built (TASK-125): the rules hold up to 21 weekly slots (weekday, from, to, a name) that members read; sessions are still scheduled one by one or as a weekly series. Defaults until a club sets its own: check-in opens 30 minutes before the start, closes 30 minutes after, late after 10 minutes, 300 m. The radius is 25 to 300 m. A session's place is its own when staff moved it, else the usual place, else the venue of its tables; when none has a position, nobody checks in by phone and staff mark by hand. A member who is invited or coming may check in; one who declined may not. The position travels in the request body for that one check, is never stored or logged, and check-in is rate-limited like a search by position; the web app asks the device for a fresh fix. Members see a place by name; its coordinates are for training staff. A mark made by staff replaces a check-in, and the record says which it was and whether the member was late.

### REQ-053

**Order of play and table flow — Phase 1 (user request, 2026-11-21).** Competition staff publish a tournament's order of play: the sequence of event stages, for example men's singles group stage groups A to E, then women's singles groups, then men's singles knockout. Everyone who sees the tournament sees it. Officials reorder it during the event, and each change is logged with who made it and when.

*No empty tables.* Dispatch (REQ-043) fills each free table with the next ready match in the order of play. If the current stage has no ready match, it takes one from the next stage whose players are free, so no table stands empty while a match could be played. The control centre shows idle tables and their idle time, and the day's table use.

*Next match.* When the umpire's deck reaches the last game's winning point but the result isn't confirmed yet, the app recommends the match that will take that table next and shows it to staff (and to the umpire after confirming), so the next players can be called early. The recommendation never assigns the table or confirms anything by itself.

*Release and late starts (user request, 2026-11-22).* The order of play is ready before the tournament starts. It goes public at a time staff choose, from three days before play starts up to the start; by default three days before. Until then only staff see planned times, and every player entered is told when it goes out. Staff move a match later, or set that a player can't play before a time (all of their matches from 11:00 instead of 09:00): the matches move and are published at once with whatever then clashes, the people affected are told, and dispatch holds that player's matches until their time unless staff set it aside with a reason.

*Tournament and table umpires (user request, 2026-11-22).* Competition staff list who umpires at a tournament: members of the host and players entered in it, so players umpire when they have no match. Being listed lets them score the tournament's matches. Staff give each table one umpire for the tournament, and every match sent to that table takes that umpire at once, so the umpire is set before the players arrive. Staff change a table's umpire at any time; the match waiting on the table follows unless scoring has started. A table umpire playing in the match, or playing elsewhere at that moment, is skipped and staff choose, and a player-umpire's own match waits while they umpire.

### REQ-054

**Club penalties for absences — Phase 1 (user request, 2026-11-21).** Club officials (governance staff) set penalty rules for missing what a member signed up for or was picked for. This covers an accepted tournament entry or a representative squad place not attended (no-show or not checked in), and training sessions accepted but missed without an apology. The meeting rules of TASK-121 cover meetings. Each rule has a penalty (warning, suspension from selection for a number of events, or points) and an optional fine through the REQ-022 ledger. Apologies sent in time (REQ-052) excuse an absence when the rule allows it. Penalties are proposed by the app and issued or waived by staff with a reason; the member sees their own penalties and can appeal once.

As built (TASK-136, REQ-043): a published announcement, and the tournament's whole sheet of published announcements oldest first, download as a PDF for whoever may see the tournament; a draft's PDF is for the host's staff.

As built (TASK-129): one rule per kind of absence, and penalties copy the rule as it stood when proposed. The app looks back 90 days, at active members only, and proposes each absence once: a training session the member accepted and staff marked them absent from (also one they apologised for, when the club says an apology doesn't excuse); an accepted entry in one of the club's own tournaments where neither the entry nor the player was checked in, once the tournament is over; an accepted squad place where staff recorded afterwards that the player didn't go. A proposed penalty is not shown to the member. Issuing tells the member and, when the rule has a fine, creates a charge in the ledger; waiving needs a reason and waives that charge. The member appeals an issued penalty once; governance staff uphold it (which waives the penalty) or turn it down. ### REQ-055

**Tournament reviews and feedback forms — Phase 1 (user request, 2026-11-21).** After a tournament the host's competition staff record a review: what went well, what went wrong and actions for next time, with owners. The review is visible to the host's staff and officials.

As built (TASK-130): one review per tournament, written from its last day (or once it is completed) and replaced each time it is saved, with who last changed it and when. Each action has a text, an owner written as a name (they may not have an account), an optional due date and whether it is done. Officials read it and can't change it.

*Feedback forms.* Staff build feedback forms with a form builder. A form has ordered sections and questions of configurable types: rating (1 to 5), single choice, multiple choice, short text, long text, yes/no and number. Each question can be required and can have help text. Forms can be saved as templates and reused. A form is sent to a tournament's attendees: players with accepted entries, umpires, staff, and optionally named guests. It opens and closes on set dates, and can be anonymous. Each attendee answers once and can edit their answers until the form closes. Staff see live totals per question (averages, counts per choice, text answers) and export the answers as CSV. As built (TASK-131): a form belongs to the host and to one tournament; a template belongs to the host only and is copied, never sent. Sections, questions and choices carry ids given by the API, and answers are stored by question id. Sending fixes the questions and who is asked: players of accepted entries, the tournament's umpires, the host's competition staff and named guests (members or listed players), as chosen on the form; they are told in the app. The form opens now or at a set time and closes at a set time at most 180 days later; staff may move the closing time, also to open it again. House rules where the statutes leave the choice to the organizer:

- Groups: seeds are spread in a snake (seed 1 to group A, 2 to B, …, then back), and the remaining entries are drawn into the same snake, so group sizes differ by one at most.
- Groups to knockout: group winners take the seeded places of the knockout in group order (winner of A as seed 1, B as seed 2, …); byes go to the best qualifiers first; a lower qualifier is drawn into the half away from the winner of their own group when there is room, and never straight into a first-round match against their own group while another place is open.
- Unplayed and unfinished matches in the game and point ratios (the statutes give the match points only): the match is completed in the winner's favour. A walkover counts as the games needed at 11–0; in a retirement or a disqualification the games played stand, the game in progress goes to the winner at 11 (or two clear) and the games still needed at 11–0; the disqualified side scores no match point. **Owner to confirm** against the ITTF Handbook for Tournament Referees.
- Team ties in group tables: individual matches won and lost are the rubbers decided; games and points are those of the rubbers decided, a walkover or unfinished rubber completed in its winner's favour as above. A tie awarded without being played, or awarded part-way because a team could not go on, is completed in the winner's favour: the wins still needed, each in straight games at 11–0. The wins needed are at most half the rubbers rounded up, so a tie always has a winner. **Owner to confirm.**
- Standings rank groups only. A knockout shows the winner, the runner-up and who went out in each finished round; nothing else is ranked.
- A finished group writes its qualifiers into the knockout places waiting for them; a qualifier with a bye goes straight to the next round.
- The lot is drawn from a recorded seed number, so the same entries, seeds and number always give the same draw.
